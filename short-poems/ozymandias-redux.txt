I met a traveller flying business class,
Who said—“Two vast and floodlit legs of stone
Stand in a plaza . . . . Near the underpass,
A shattered visage scowls at every phone
Whose chiselled lip and sneer recast in glass
Tell that the market well his legend saved
Which yet survives, stamped on the plastic things,
The airport novels and the roads he paved;
And on the pedestal, these words appear:
‘My name is Ozymandias, King of Kings;
Look on my Works, ye Mighty, and despair!’
Much else beside remains. Some hours away,
Preserved beneath the Grand Museum’s glare,
The pharaoh draws ten thousand guests a day.”
